QC reviewer fatigue: QC reviewers themselves show declining error detection after 50+ decisions in a session. Risk-based QC sampling should prioritise: (1) decisions made late in analyst sessions, (2) decisions by analysts with recent quality issues, (3) cases at the boundary of escalation thresholds — rather than purely random sampling.

3. Correspondent Banking

Products: Correspondent Banking Account, Payable-Through Account
Applicable auto scenarios: 7 of 26 — GJ-01, GJ-02, GJ-03, RM-01, RM-02, SP-01, SP-02
Obligations: OBL-CORR-001 (Due Diligence), OBL-CORR-002 (Shell Bank Prohibition), OBL-AML-002 (EDD), OBL-EDD-003 (Correspondent EDD)

Correspondent banking TM is fundamentally different from retail. The unit of monitoring is the respondent relationship, not the individual customer. Alerts fire on aggregated flows through the nostro/vostro account, with underlying originator/beneficiary data often incomplete (pre-SWIFT MT→MX migration).

Why correspondent is 2–3x slower than retail:
  1. Information asymmetry: The correspondent sees flows but has limited visibility into the respondent’s underlying customers. Investigation often requires requesting information from the respondent — introducing a 5–15 BD communication delay.
  2. Nested relationships: A Payable-Through Account may have thousands of end-users. Identifying which underlying customer triggered the pattern requires SWIFT message parsing and respondent cooperation.
  3. Multi-jurisdictional complexity: A single suspicious flow may transit 3–4 jurisdictions with different regulatory frameworks, sanctions regimes, and reporting requirements.
  4. Relationship management overlay: Filing a SAR on a respondent has significant commercial implications. Decisions typically require sign-off from relationship management, legal, and senior compliance — adding 3–5 BD to the decision chain.

5. Enforcement Case Lessons

Case Product Focus TM/SAR Failure QC/QA Lesson
Danske Bank ($2B × 2) Correspondent $230B suspicious flows through correspondent accounts, systemic AML failures, inadequate SAR reporting Per-respondent TM calibration was absent; volume-based scenarios missed pattern-based laundering
HSBC ($1.9B × 2) Correspondent Retail Failed AML programme, drug cartel funds via correspondent, sanctioned country processing Correspondent exit decisions delayed by commercial pressure; manual referrals from compliance not acted upon
TD Bank ($3.1B) Retail Employee facilitation, $670M laundered, BSA violations 1st line QC didn’t catch employee collusion; auto alerts on high-volume accounts systematically closed

Binance ($4.3B) Retail Willful failure to implement AML programme, >100K suspicious transactions unprocessed No QC/QA framework existed — fundamental programme absence rather than control failure
ING (€775M × 2) Retail Failed to vet customers, monitor activities, report unusual transactions for years Neither auto detection nor manual referral processes were functioning — structural shortcomings
CBA (AUD 700M × 2) Retail 53,506 threshold transaction reporting failures, late suspicious matter reports Auto-threshold alerts existed but weren’t actioned — a pure QC failure on disposition backlog

6.2 Workload & Fatigue Management

Auto Alerts

  • Vigilance decrement: 15–20 min degradation on threshold/frequency alerts
  • Decision fatigue: Quality deteriorates through sessions — schedule high-risk at shift start
  • Rotation: Mandatory every 30–45 min (retail) / 45–60 min (correspondent)
  • QC reviewer limit: Declining error detection after 50+ decisions per session

Manual Alerts

  • Variety effect: Cognitive variety from unstructured cases maintains engagement
  • Interleaving: Mix manual cases between auto-alert batches to reset cognitive state
  • Information assembly fatigue: Unstructured referrals impose high cognitive load on assembly
  • Structured forms: Mandatory referral fields reduce assembly time 40–60%

6.3 Feedback Loops

  • Closed-loop learning: Every alert disposition should link to an outcome (SAR decision, case closure). Analysts who never learn outcomes plateau at novice-level pattern recognition regardless of experience.
  • False positive tuning: FP dispositions should feed screening engine tuning — whitelisting rules, threshold adjustment, list entry enrichment.
  • SAR outcome feedback: Where legally permitted, aggregated outcome data (“X% of SARs from this team contributed to active investigations”) reinforces purpose and combats disengagement.
  • Risk rating calibration: Track SAR rates by risk tier — if medium-risk generates more SARs per capita than high-risk, the model is miscalibrated.

6.4 Commercial Pressure — Correspondent Banking

Severe risk: Respondent relationship revenue creates pressure against SAR filing and relationship exit decisions. QC must specifically check for under-investigation on high-revenue relationships. This was a primary finding in the Danske Bank, HSBC, and Swedbank enforcement cases. Correspondent banking QA should include an annual review correlating investigation intensity with respondent revenue — any negative correlation is a control failure.
